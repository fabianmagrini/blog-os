# Audience

## Primary readers

- senior software engineers
- principal engineers
- staff engineers
- architects
- engineering managers
- engineering leaders
- heads of engineering
- CTOs
- platform engineers
- technical product leaders

## Assumptions about the reader

- They have shipped production systems and made architectural trade-offs
  under real constraints.
- They are skeptical of hype and can detect when a claim is unsupported.
- They have likely already encountered the surface-level version of most
  topics on this list — do not re-explain fundamentals unless the argument
  depends on a specific, possibly-contested detail of the fundamental.
- They read to update their own mental model or decision-making, not for
  entertainment or reassurance.
- They will forward or cite an article only if it says something specific
  enough to disagree with.

## What this audience wants from an article

- a clear, falsifiable point of view — not a balanced survey
- evidence, not just assertion
- named trade-offs, not just benefits
- acknowledgment of the strongest counterargument
- concrete detail (real architectures, real failure modes, real numbers)
  over generic abstraction

## What to avoid

- explaining what a PR, a container, or an LLM is
- padding with background the reader almost certainly already has
- writing as if the reader needs to be convinced technology matters at all
